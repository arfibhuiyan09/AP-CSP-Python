## Discussion Questions:

**Question 1: Do you think the ability to make money or save time justifies ethically questionable uses of AI?**

    Somewhat: The ethicality of AI assisting to save time or money depends on what the action is.
    For instance, in Scenario 16, a clickbait blogger wants to save time to spread more clickbait; 
    that is an unethical use of AI as it could cause more people to fall for the "AI-Slop" articles.
    However, if that creator used their platform to spread credible information instead of clickbaiting,
    that would be a ethical usage of AI (with drawbacks) since its intent is to help and inform the masses.

**Question 2: Should AI be entrusted with life-or-death decisions?**

    AI should be trusted with drastic, life-or-death scenarios can react much faster than a human can.
    Machines can perform up to billions of calculations in a thousand of a second (1ms);
    that is 100 times faster than the time it takes an average human to blink (100ms). 
    This shows that AI in self-driving velichles such as Teslas or Waymos are always aware,
    reacting to everything; if a single thing is unnormal, it will react immediately (unlike a human driver).

**Question 3: What made some scenarios more challenging to classify?**

    Scenarios like Scenario 3 was hard to classify due to its intent to help facilitate the spread of knowledge,
    but using a robot rather than a human.
    Although the AI can create beautiful lesson ideas and the teacher revises it, checking for any misinformation,

    This can be marked as a Level 4: Ethical; With Drawbacks since it helps the students and facilitates learning,
